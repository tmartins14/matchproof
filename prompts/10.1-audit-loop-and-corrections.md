On Azure today, the independent auditor (`judge-grok`) disagreed with the inline judge (`gpt-5-mini`) on 3 of 9 claims, and it was right each time:

| Claim (verified inline) | Auditor's finding |
| --- | --- |
| "Pryce scores for Rivermouth, a smart finish after a 6-second move to make it 2–0." | `overclaim`: "smart" is evaluative |
| "…Ashford Vale have pushed their back line 4 metres higher since the goal, leaving more space across the pitch." | `unsupported_cause` |
| "…while Ashford's share of play has risen to 53%." | `overclaim`: the fact is opposition-half share, and "risen" has no before fact |

This morning's live run had three more: "edge in front", "tighter", "composed".

Banning words one at a time won't scale. This task closes the loop in two ways:
1. Every auditor disagreement becomes a permanent eval case, and the judge prompt learns from them.
2. When the auditor disagrees, a consensus re-check can correct a claim that's already on air, visibly and honestly.

Before writing: restate the task back in eight lines or fewer. Include the exact consensus rule and what the viewer sees on a correction. Then wait for my go.

## Parts

1. **Disagreements become eval cases.**
   - Add the six disagreements above to `evals/adversarial/claims.yaml` as bad claims, tied to real beats with the cited facts as in the live runs, each with expected `rephrased` or `blocked`.
   - Add 4 near-miss good claims that should still pass, for example a vivid action verb with a supported outcome, or "Rivermouth lead 2–0" with the lead fact cited.
   - The set becomes 44 bad and 18 good. Scale the pick rule: at least 41 of 44 caught, at most 1 of 18 good blocked, judge p90 at most 4 s.
   - Add `evals/from_audit.py`: it reads `/matches/{id}/audit` (or an archived `audit.json`) and prints disagreements as ready-to-paste YAML eval entries, with the beat id and fact ids filled in. Document it as the routine: "every auditor disagreement becomes an eval case".
2. **`comparatives.yaml` and adjectives.** Add `risen`, `rose`, `fallen`, `fell`, `increased`, `decreased`, `grown`, `shrunk`, `more space`, `less space` to comparatives, and `smart` to the banned evaluative adjectives. This is the cheap, deterministic part; the next part is the real fix.
3. **Judge v3 (`judge.v3.md`).** Keep v2's rubric and add a short "commonly missed" section with one-line examples of each failure type from the table: an evaluative adjective, a consequence or cause clause ("leaving…", "which means…", "so…"), a fact relabelled (opposition-half share called "share of play"), and a change word with no before fact. Re-run `make bakeoff` (gpt-5-mini and judge-grok are enough; skip judge-mai). **Target:** gpt-5-mini catches at least 41 of 44, blocks at most 1 of 18, p90 at most 4 s. If it can't, report the misses; don't weaken the rule.
4. **Corrections by consensus (`agents/consensus.py`, live wiring).**
   - **Trigger:** the auditor returns `disagree` on a claim whose verdict was `verified` or `rephrased`.
   - **Re-check:** run the inline judge (v3) again on the same claim and facts, with the auditor's problem and reason added as "a second reviewer raised this; decide if it is right".
   - **Consensus rule:**
     - if the re-check now agrees with the auditor, the claim is **corrected**: send the claim to the Narrator once (the existing revise path) with both reasons. If the new wording passes the full check (reconcile, lint, judge), the new text replaces the old with verdict `rephrased`. If it can't be fixed, the verdict becomes `blocked`;
     - if the re-check still disagrees with the auditor, nothing changes on screen, and the disagreement is logged as unresolved.
   - At most one correction per claim. Corrections count toward the token cap, and stop when auditing stops.
   - **Schema:** add an optional `correction` object to `claim.schema.json` with `at_t` (match time), `previous_text`, `reason` (the auditor's reason) and `by` (`["judge-grok", "gpt-5-mini"]`). Run `make gen`.
   - **Stream:** a correction is sent as a `verdict` message with the new verdict and text plus the `correction` object, so the UI can show it as a broadcast-style correction. Never silently rewrite a claim already on screen; the previous text is always in the message.
   - **Audit summary:** add `corrections`, `unresolved` and an agreement rate after corrections.
5. **`/health` reports agent status:** `{"status": "ok", "agents": "ready"}`, or `"agents": "unavailable: <reason>"`. The container smoke test checks the field exists.
6. **Docs:**
   - ARCHITECTURE: the consensus diagram (writer, inline judge, auditor, re-check), plus "every auditor disagreement becomes an eval case".
   - ADR 0002 Decision log: judge v3 and its bake-off numbers, plus the correction rule.
   - VOICE.md: how a correction reads on screen (a CORRECTED label, the previous text struck through, the reason).

## Tests (CI, no model calls; fake models)

- **Consensus paths:**
  - auditor disagrees and re-check agrees with it → corrected, revised text sent with a `correction` object;
  - revision fails the check → blocked, with `correction`;
  - re-check disagrees with the auditor → nothing sent, logged unresolved;
  - auditor agrees → no re-check call (assert the fake was not called).
- At most one correction per claim; corrections stop when the cap trips.
- The `correction` message always carries `previous_text`, and it validates against the schema.
- `from_audit.py` turns a fake audit into valid eval YAML.
- Comparatives: "share … has risen to 53%" with only a since fact fails reconciliation.
- `/health` includes `agents`.
